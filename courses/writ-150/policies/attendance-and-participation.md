# WRIT 150 attendance and participation

Source: `../syllabus/2026-fall-writ150-syllabus-verbatim.md`, numbered policies 1 and 2 plus
the grading breakdown notes.

## Attendance

- 3 absences are free, per Writing Program policy.
- Absence 4 and beyond: the **course grade** drops a half grade for each one. Not the
  participation grade, the course grade.
- Exceptions: university sanctioned absences such as athletic events, and medical
  emergencies.
- **More than 9 absences fails the course**, per university policy. That is 3 weeks of a
  MWF schedule.
- Work is still due on time whether or not class was attended.
- After an absence, get notes and handouts from a classmate.
- Do not attend while contagious. Contact the instructor to arrange continued participation.

A missed conference counts as an absence **and** lowers participation. That is a double hit.

## Tardiness

- No formal late-to-class penalty.
- Excessive lateness, meaning more than 4 or 5 times, may lower participation at her
  discretion.
- More than 20 minutes late counts as an **absence**. Still attend, so as not to miss the
  material.
- **If arriving after attendance is taken, it is the student's job to walk up to her and get
  marked tardy instead of absent.** This is on the student, not on her.

## Participation, 5% of the grade

Starts at an A. Drops a half step for each of:
- A missed peer review.
- A missed conference.
- Excessive tardiness.
- Disruptive behavior, which she names as sleeping in class and repeated lack of preparedness.

Explicit expectations from the syllabus: no phones or laptops for messaging, browsing, or
other coursework during class. Step out for an important call. Do not sleep. It is a
seminar, not a lecture hall.

## Service hours penalty, separate from everything above

Every 2 hours of the required 12 not completed drops the grade a half step. An A becomes an
A minus, a B minus becomes a C plus. Missing all 12 hours is a 6 half-step drop.

## Things that do not require asking

She states these directly: leaving for the bathroom without asking, eating a snack in class,
saying "pass" when called on, or telling her ahead of time that today is not a good day.
