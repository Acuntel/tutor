# WRIT 150 AI policy

Verbatim source: `../syllabus/2026-fall-writ150-syllabus-verbatim.md`, section "AI Policy".
This is the binding rule for this course. Read it before any work that touches a WP or AWA.

## The instructor's exact words

> AI such as ChatGPT, Copilot, Claude, Gemini, Grammarly and other applications are not
> permitted for any portion of your writing.

> You may use a spellchecker or grammar checker to identify and correct potential errors,
> but you may not use any application to re-write or polish your sentences to "make it sound
> better."

> I hope that you will not use AI to brainstorm ideas, though you may do so as long as you
> cite the AI application you've used and how you've used it.

> Similarly, I hope that you don't use AI to find sources, but if you do so, you must also
> cite the AI application you've used, and you must check that these sources are REAL.

And from the academic integrity section:

> Students may not have another person or entity complete any substantive portion of the
> assignment, including revision or editing.

## Penalties

| Where AI is found | Consequence |
|---|---|
| On an AWA | Warning, and the AWA must be redone |
| On WP1 or WP2 | Mandatory rewrite, grade drops 3 half steps (A becomes B), reported to the Office of Academic Integrity |
| On WP3 | **Fail the course**, reported to the Office of Academic Integrity |

A student found to have used AI also loses access to the one allowed rewrite.

Suspicion alone is enough to trigger a report. The syllabus says adjudication happens at
the Office of Academic Integrity, not in the classroom.

## Operational rules for this repo

**Forbidden. Do not do these, ever, for this course.**
- Write or draft any sentence that could appear in a submitted document.
- Rewrite, polish, tighten, restructure, or improve the flow of any sentence Brady wrote.
- Revise or edit a draft. "Revision or editing" is named explicitly in the syllabus.
- Produce a thesis statement, an outline, or topic sentences for Brady to fill in.
- Suggest specific wording intended to be typed into the paper.
- Generate a counterargument paragraph, a hook, a transition, or a conclusion.

**Allowed and useful.**
- Logistics, deadlines, policy lookups, grade math.
- Explaining what a prompt is asking for, in the prompt's own words.
- Explaining a concept or a reading so Brady understands it before he writes.
- Asking Socratic questions about his argument so he finds the gap himself.
- Mechanical compliance checks on a finished draft: font, spacing, margins, header format,
  page numbers, presence of required elements, MLA citation formatting.
- Verifying a source exists, that a quote is accurate, and what its correct citation is.
- Tracking state: what is done, what is outstanding, what is due next.

**Allowed only with a written citation in the submitted work.**
- Brainstorming. Permitted by the syllabus if the application and the manner of use are
  cited. If this is ever used, log it in the relevant file under `../assignments/` so the
  citation can be written accurately, and keep in mind the instructor said she hopes it is
  not used.
- Source finding. Same citation requirement, plus mandatory verification that every source
  is real. The syllabus warns about hallucinated sources directly.

## The sanctioned alternative

The USC Writing Center in THH 216 does exactly what AI is banned from doing here, and the
syllabus states plainly that using it is not a violation. One 1-hour session or two
half-hour sessions per week. Bring the assignment, all drafts and prewriting, and specific
questions. For anything that would otherwise be "help me make this better," that is the
route.
