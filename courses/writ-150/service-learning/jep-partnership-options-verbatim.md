Prison Education Project (PEP) Readers' Circle/The Social Impact Center

This joint partnership gives students the opportunity to volunteer with both the USC Dornsife Prison Education Project (PEP) Readers' Circle and the Social Impact Center.

The USC Dornsife Prison Education Project (PEP) Readers' Circle is a program within the broader USC Dornsife PEP organization and currently consists of over 300 student and faculty volunteer editors who regularly proofread, edit, and provide feedback to incarcerated authors. Each year, they support hundreds of writers who are currently in prison. They are looking for volunteers who can commit to helping with tasks such: digitizing/typing handwritten manuscripts from authors; providing editorial feedback to authors; and preparing edited manuscripts for return. Volunteer work can be completed both asynchronously and/or during Readers' Circle "scanning parties" on campus.
The Social Impact Center works in some of Los Angeles's most marginalized communities (which have been disparately impacted by the War on Drugs) by serving as a hub for grassroots community organizers. They provide an array of services to impacted community members, including direct expungement services to formerly incarcerated individuals. In order to effectively reach their populations, they take a community-first approach to service delivery by going directly into communities and partnering with neighborhood institutions like churches, cultural centers, and other community-based organizations. They hold events in public spaces that are accessible to communities experiencing poverty and lack of resources like parks, spaces close to public transit, and in coordination with local community events. They are looking for volunteers for their expungement events to help justice-involved community members draft declarations in support of their expungement petitions.

(An expungement is a chance for an individual with a conviction to clear their record and escape discrimination in housing and employment that stems from their criminal history. These declarations are their chance to explain their situation and how much they've changed since their conviction. This is powerful evidence that has often been cited as the deciding factor in a judge's decision to grant an expungement.)

As volunteers for this joint partnership, students will be expected to complete a combination of asynchronous online tasks and/or work at in-person "scanning parties" for the Readers' Circle as well as participate as part of hybrid weekend expungement events/project support for the Social Impact Center (these events will include both in-person and virtual options). Dates for expungement events for Fall 2026 will be forthcoming shortly.

To sum up, this partnership has both asynchronous and synchronous virtual options for volunteers as well as in-person opportunities.

FoodCycle LA

FoodCycle LA recovers and diverts surplus food to communities experiencing food insecurity. They partner with food retailers to reroute surplus food away from landfills to organizations that feed hungry people. FoodCycle LA is also dedicated to introducing innovative technology into the food recovery space. FoodCycle LA introduced the Careit APP to increase their impact and better recover and route excess food; collaborating with Food Oasis, they created a database of organizations that are serving hungry people to move resources into the food deserts of LA where the need is greatest.

Students partnering with FoodCycle have two main ways they can volunteer.
The most common is to sign up to volunteer with FoodCycle's partners - these are the organizations which distribute the recovered food to community members through regularly scheduled events. In addition to packaging and directly serving food to local communities, students will also document the food distribution for Food Cycle through photos, videos and short written pieces. Food Cycle will then use this documentation in their social media, newsletters and other communications to share the impact that their partners have, and how FoodCycle supports them.
Students can also work directly with FoodCycle. Students who drive and have consistent access to a car can sign up as a driver, picking up surplus food and taking it to partner sites. All students can also sign up (through the same site) to glean farmer's markets - collecting and packaging fresh food that would otherwise go wasted, allowing it to be redistributed at partner sites. These opportunities may be an especially worthwhile form of service for students interested in the climate change and sustainability aspects of FoodCycle's work.

Water Drop LA

Water Drop aims to support existing organizers and groups and to meet the immediate needs of the community. The organization believes that access to clean drinking water is a fundamental human right and that legislative advocacy must be accompanied by a swift response to critical necessities. Drops begin by loading supplies and then driving in groups to Skid Row for distribution.

Alternatives
If you are already working with a community partnership to enhance opportunities and equity, WE HIGHLY ENCOURAGE YOU TO CONTINUE WITH THIS PARTNERSHIP TO FULFILL THE COURSE REQUIREMENT!  Partnerships are often most impactful and meaningful for all participants when volunteers are invested in and have relationships with the communities they work with. If there is a local community or organization you have wanted to engage with or have already been involved with in some capacity, please use this course as an opportunity to explore and/or deepen that partnership.

As we will discuss in the class, community engagement DOES NOT have to conform to direct forms of service or service to a community that is somehow new or separate from the communities you already belong to. Activism and serving one's home communities are valid forms of engagement that still require intentionality and reflection when navigating power. In some ways, these forms of engagement can actually be more challenging as they require us to organize on institutional scales (say a political campaign) or deeply personal scales (say tutoring a sibling).

However, not all forms of volunteering engage in community – indeed, common volunteer activities (say, a one-day beach clean-up, or a single day serving food in a homeless shelter) can be highly problematic when they are performative and reinforce harmful power dynamics and privileges. Students who wish to propose alternatives should carefully consider the needs of their communities, the people they wish to engage, and the long-term sustainability/outcomes of those efforts. Note: the focus must be engaging with a HUMAN community, which means that volunteer opportunities focused on ANIMALS (such as shelters) generally would not be an appropriate choice for this course.
