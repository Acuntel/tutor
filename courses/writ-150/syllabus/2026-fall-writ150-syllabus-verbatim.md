Course Description: Welcome to Writing 150! This course is designed to teach critical thinking, reading, and writing strategies for academic success. We will focus on writing as a process as we learn various tools for invention (pre-writing and brainstorming), drafting, and revision. Writing is a recursive practice, which means that it is not an acquired skill that you learn through one essay assignment or even in one semester. Instead, your writing—and thinking—grows sharper and stronger with every deliberate act of the written word.

While we will touch upon various rhetorical modes of writing, our major Writing Projects (WPs) will be argumentative in nature and require you to develop and support an original thesis based on your own inquiry. Toward that end, you will have the opportunity to consider how to create strong arguments and support your claims, how to conduct and integrate credible research, how to write toward an audience, and how to organize and extend your ideas with the style and sophistication appropriate for college-level writing. Over the course of the semester, you will complete 3 WPs (the USC way of saying essays!) culminating in a Final Portfolio. As part of this work, you will be asked to complete smaller writing assignments that we call Ancillary Writing (AWAs) in service to your given WP.

The course thematic of Community Engagement is unique in that it requires a service-learning component. By signing up for this course, you have committed to volunteering for a minimum of 12 hours on your own time in a community organization with which we are partnered. This volunteer work is first and foremost a way to positively contribute to the surrounding communities of greater Los Angeles. It is also an opportunity to not only study/research various topics around social inequity but to gain firsthand perspective and insight into the causes, symptoms, and repercussions of such inequities and the systems of power that uphold them. Further, you will be asked to think deeply and critically of your own relationships of power and privilege or lack thereof in connection with such issues.

Assignments:
 Writing Projects: You will write 3 major essays called Writing Projects (WPs) and one smaller assignment (WP3a). You are required to turn in all WPs including WP3a in order to pass the class.

Because of this, be mindful that you must be willing to share what you write for this class with others and steer clear of anything too personal that you aren’t interested in other people knowing.

Writing Projects: Rough drafts due on scheduled peer review sessions will be counted as Ancillary Writing Assignments and must be typed. Final drafts must be formatted in MLA style. Follow these basic parameters to avoid deductions:
• use ONLY Times New Roman, 12-point font (NOT in bold, italic or script form)
• double space writings with 1-inch margins all around.
• do not skip spaces between paragraphs.
• all essays should also be originally titled in the same font/size as noted above.
• all writings must include a header in the upper left-hand corner of the first page listing your name, the instructor’s name, the course number (Writ 150) and the date.
• also include a header in the upper right-hand corner on each page of your essay with your last name and the page number.
For more details on MLA Style and Formatting, I recommend the Purdue University OWL guide, in particular, their Sample Paper is very useful.

On procrastination: College can be both fun and scary. There is so much to take in, and for some of you, you are managing your time in a whole new way. As someone who has suffered from procrastination as a student and a writer, I can share that so much of procrastination comes from a fear of failure or a desire to be perfect on a first try. This is a lifelong lesson, not something you’ll learn in one class—something that continues to challenge me. But I encourage you to be brave in the face of potential failure. Surrender to the possibility and take the risk. There is something to failing well—when you fail well, you haven’t failed because you can honestly say you put in your best effort, and that “failure” will teach you how to move forward.

Anti-bias Statement written by colleagues in the Writing Program:
We are dedicated to anti-bias in and out of the classroom. We wholeheartedly stand against racism, dehumanization, oppression, colonization, xenophobia, and white supremacy. Silence on matters of injustice is not an option.
We acknowledge that oppression and dehumanization can occur consciously and unconsciously; it is important to have patience with each other. But we must also commit to the lifelong work of deconstructing our unconscious and conscious biases; dismantling racist institutions; and creating an academic environment that rejects white supremacy in all its forms.
Even further, academic writing has historically been a tool for oppression. It has encouraged standardization originally based in whiteness – and so we must ask, “Who set those standards? Why do we value only those standards? And as writers, how can we reform the standards?” Anti-racism/bias is, in fact, intrinsic to the goals of multimodal, multilingual, and decolonized writing and research.
As a writing class, we will actively engage in self-reflection to look inward and examine where our conscious and unconscious biases are rooted. We will encourage and support each other’s journeys. We will cultivate authentic voices; interrogate our policies and practices; and commit to continually educating ourselves to provide an educational experience that is anti-racist, humanized, and celebrates intersectional identities. With collaboration, we will create an anti-bias classroom.

5. “Late Paper Pass”: Each student may have one (and only one) late paper pass if you fail to turn one of your essays in on time. This is a 3-day extension for your essay without penalty. It is best to contact me in advance of the due date, but it is not necessary. However, you must let me know that you will be using your Late Paper Pass.

6. WP Re-Writes: Each student is allowed one re-write. If you are dissatisfied with a grade that you receive on a Writing Project, you may choose to re-write the paper for a higher grade. However, you must first read all the comments and write a 2-3 paragraph revision plan to show that you’ve understood the feedback, and you have an idea of how you might improve the paper. Alternatively, you can conference with me to approve your verbal revision plan. Once I approve the revision plan, you can proceed to re-write the paper. Whatever grade I give the re-write will replace the original. You have 2 weeks from the date of return to opt into a re-write. Please note that re-writing a paper does not guarantee you a higher grade. Re-writing is not an option for students who’ve been found to use AI or otherwise cheat on their essay.

Academic Integrity: Need I say it? Don’t plagiarize! Any form of presenting someone else’s words or ideas as your own is not tolerated in the academic community and will result in serious penalties. It is your responsibility to be aware of the policies regarding academic integrity in the USC Campus Student Handbook. You are responsible and accountable to all forms of plagiarism including the use of AI whether you plagiarize intentionally or not. Plagiarism may result in failure of assignment, failure of the course, academic probation or expulsion. Plagiarism is serious, and all suspected plagiarism cases will be referred to the Associate Director of the Writing Program as well as USC Student Judicial Affairs and Community Standards. For more information visit: Research and Scholarship Misconduct.

You commit plagiarism if you do ANY of the following
• Use the internet or another source to “get ideas” that you use without citing.
• “Cut and paste” or modify and include text, images, or sound in a project without citing.
• Put another person’s ideas “in your own words” without documenting the source.
• Submit a project created by someone else, including a tutor, while claiming to be the author.
• Submit a project created in whole or part for another course (including high school) without the permission of both instructors.

AI Policy
As all of you now have access to a school-sponosored Chatgpt account, it is worth discussing an AI policy.
• AI such as ChatGPT, Copilot, Claude, Gemini, Grammarly and other applications are not permitted for any portion of your writing.
• You may use a spellchecker or grammar checker to identify and correct potential errors, but you may not use any application to re-write or polish your sentences to “make it sound better.” TRUST ME; it never sounds better!
• I hope that you will not use AI to brainstorm ideas, though you may do so as long as you cite the AI application you’ve used and how you’ve used it.
• Similarly, I hope that you don’t use AI to find sources, but if you do so, you must also cite the AI application you’ve used, and you must check that these sources are REAL. AI is often wrong and can hallucinate sources. The due diligence required here creates more work for you than doing your research through the library databases.
• If you are found to have used AI on an AWA, you will be given a warning and asked to re-do it.
• If you are found to have used AI on WP1 or WP2, you will be required to re-write it, and your grade for the paper will go down by 3 half steps. Ie. A to B. You will also be reported to the Office of Academic Integrity.
• If you are found to have used AI on your final WP3, you will fail the course and be reported to the Office of Academic Integrity.
• While AI is not always detectable with 100% certainty, there are some common and obvious indicators. Suspicion of AI will be reported and adjudicated by the Office of Academic Integrity.

Your writing should be a reflection of your original thought, both in content and form. Since creating, analytical, and critical thinking skills are part of the learning outcomes of this course, all assignments should be prepared by the student working individually or in groups. Students may not have another person or entity complete any substantive portion of the assignment, including revision or editing. Developing strong competencies in these areas will prepare you for a competitive workplace.
